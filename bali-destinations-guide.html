The Ultimate Bali Area Guide

A Local's Guide to Exploring the Island of the Gods

Welcome to Bali! Known as the "Island of the Gods," Bali is not just a single destination—it is a vast, diverse island made up of distinct regions, each with its own unique personality, landscape, and vibe. As a first-time visitor, looking at a map of Bali can be incredibly overwhelming. Should you stay in the bustling beach towns of the south, retreat to the jungle, or cross the ocean to the neighboring islands?

To help you plan the perfect itinerary, our local experts at Bali Real Vacation have broken down the island’s absolute best regions. We cover the highlights, the vibe, and practical logistics to ensure your tropical getaway is flawless.

1. Logistics: Getting Around the Island

Before diving into where to go, you need to know how to get there. Bali's public transportation is virtually non-existent, and the traffic—especially in the southern hubs like Canggu and Seminyak—can be notoriously chaotic.

  • Scooter Rentals: While popular among long-term backpackers, renting a scooter is highly discouraged for inexperienced riders. The winding roads, steep hills, and aggressive traffic lead to countless tourist accidents every year.
  • Taxis & Ride-Hailing Apps: Apps like Grab and Gojek are great for short, quick trips within a single town. However, many local areas and popular tourist sites ban ride-hailing apps from picking up passengers.
  • Private Drivers (Highly Recommended): For day trips, temple hopping, and exploring outside of your immediate hotel area, hiring a private driver is the safest, most comfortable, and most cost-effective method. You get an air-conditioned car, a local guide who speaks English, and the freedom to customize your entire day.

2. South Bali: Sunsets, Surf, and Luxury

The southern peninsula (the "foot" of Bali on the map) and the immediate southwestern coast are where you will find the highest concentration of beach clubs, luxury resorts, and world-class surfing.

Uluwatu & The Bukit Peninsula

Perched atop towering limestone cliffs, Uluwatu perfectly blends high-end luxury with rugged surf culture. The beaches here are tucked into stunning coves (like Padang Padang and Dreamland), accessed by climbing down stairs carved into the cliffs. It is less walkable than other towns, making a driver essential.

Must Do: Visit the iconic clifftop Uluwatu Temple at sunset to watch the mesmerizing Kecak Fire Dance performance overlooking the Indian Ocean.

Canggu & Seminyak

Seminyak is famous for its upscale boutiques, fine dining, and established luxury beach clubs like Potato Head. Just north of Seminyak is Canggu, the current epicenter of Bali's digital nomad scene. Canggu is vibrant, deeply trendy, and packed with aesthetic cafes, surf breaks, and late-night parties.

3. Central Bali: Ubud & The Cultural Heart

Heading inland away from the coast, you will find Ubud. Surrounded by emerald ravines, flowing rivers, and working rice paddies, Ubud is the undisputed artistic, spiritual, and cultural capital of Bali. If you are looking for yoga retreats, vegan cafes, traditional wood carving, and ancient temples, this is the place.

Top Highlights

  • Tegalalang Rice Terrace: Wander through the famously steep, incredibly photogenic terraced rice paddies.
  • Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary: Walk through a dense jungle temple complex inhabited by hundreds of wild (and cheeky!) long-tailed macaques.
  • Tirta Empul: Just outside of Ubud, visit this holy spring temple to witness or partake in a traditional Hindu water purification ritual.

4. The Highlands: North Bali & Active Volcanoes

The northern and central mountainous regions offer a stark contrast to the tropical beaches. The air is crisp and cool, the landscapes are dominated by dense jungle and crater lakes, and the region feels wonderfully undiscovered by mass tourism.

Mount Batur & Kintamani

The village of Kintamani sits on the rim of a massive volcanic caldera. Inside this caldera lies Mount Batur, an active volcano, and Lake Batur. The Mount Batur Sunrise Trek is a bucket-list activity. Hiking up the volcano under the stars to watch the sun rise above the clouds is a highly rewarding, spiritual experience.

Bedugul & The Northern Waterfalls

Bedugul is famous for the stunning Ulun Danu Beratan Temple, which appears to float on the misty waters of Lake Beratan. Further north, the terrain drops dramatically, creating Bali's most spectacular waterfalls. Sekumpul Waterfall is widely considered the most beautiful cascade on the island, requiring a thrilling jungle trek to reach the base.

5. East Bali: The Authentic Escape

East Bali remains deeply traditional. It is quieter, deeply religious, and heavily influenced by the towering presence of Mount Agung, Bali's highest and holiest volcano. Villages like Sidemen offer breathtakingly serene rice terrace views that look like Ubud did 30 years ago, while the coastal town of Amed features black volcanic sand and excellent wreck diving.

Must Do: Visit the Lempuyang Temple to capture the iconic "Gates of Heaven" photo with Mount Agung perfectly framed in the background, or wander the royal water gardens of Tirta Gangga.

6. The Neighboring Islands: Nusa Penida

Technically part of the Bali province but feeling worlds away, Nusa Penida is a rugged, relatively undeveloped island just a 45-minute fast boat ride from Sanur harbor. If you have seen viral videos of T-Rex shaped cliffs or people swimming with giant Manta Rays, it was filmed here.

Because the roads on Penida are incredibly steep and dangerous, we strongly advise against renting scooters there. Booking a guided day trip or hiring a private island driver is essential for your safety.

7. Essential Bali Travel Tips

To ensure your trip goes off without a hitch, keep these practical tips in mind:

  • Visas: Most travelers will need a Visa on Arrival (VOA) which costs roughly $35 USD. You can buy this at the airport, but we highly recommend applying for an e-VOA online before you fly to skip the massive immigration queues.
  • Currency & Cash: The currency is the Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). While hotels and fancy restaurants take cards, you need cash for local warungs (restaurants), temple donations, parking fees, and tipping your driver.
  • Cultural Etiquette: Bali is a deeply religious Hindu island. You will see small, square woven baskets containing flowers and incense (Canang Sari) on the sidewalks everywhere—do your best not to step on them! When visiting any temple, both men and women must cover their knees. A sarong is usually provided at the entrance.
  • Airport Transfers: The Denpasar (DPS) airport arrival hall is chaotic, with dozens of taxi drivers shouting for your attention. Pre-booking an airport transfer ensures a driver is holding a sign with your name, allowing you to walk right past the crowds.
